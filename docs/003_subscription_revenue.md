# Subscription and Revenue Model

The Subscription and Revenue Model of Amar School ERP is designed around a recurring Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) business philosophy, where sustainable long-term income is prioritized over one-time software sales. Instead of selling perpetual software licenses, the platform generates continuous monthly recurring revenue (MRR) by charging schools a subscription fee for using the platform. This recurring revenue model creates predictable cash flow for the company while allowing schools to pay affordable operational expenses instead of large upfront software investments. The entire commercial model is built to ensure that every stakeholder—including the company, distributors, agents, and schools—benefits from a transparent, scalable, and mutually profitable ecosystem.

The company remains the sole owner of the software, cloud infrastructure, databases, source code, licensing system, subscription platform, payment gateway, SMS gateway, and intellectual property. Schools never purchase ownership of the software; instead, they purchase the right to use the platform under an active subscription. Every subscription is centrally managed by the company, ensuring that billing, renewals, feature availability, licensing, payment processing, and financial reporting remain under centralized governance. This approach guarantees consistent service quality, simplifies financial management, and protects the long-term value of the platform.

The subscription pricing model is intentionally designed to be flexible and scalable. Rather than charging a single fixed amount for every institution, each school's monthly subscription consists of two configurable components: a fixed platform subscription fee and a variable student-based fee. Initially, the platform charges approximately **BDT 2,000 per month** as the base subscription fee, together with **BDT 7 per active student**. This pricing structure allows smaller schools to adopt the platform with a lower operational cost while ensuring that revenue naturally grows as institutions expand. Since pricing requirements may change over time, both the base subscription fee and the per-student charge must remain completely configurable through the Super Admin portal without requiring software modifications. Future pricing strategies may increase the student-based fee to **BDT 10–15 per student**, introduce different pricing tiers, or define institution-specific pricing models while preserving historical billing records.

The subscription model is closely aligned with the company's long-term growth strategy. During the initial years of market expansion, the primary objective is not maximizing profit but maximizing customer acquisition and recurring subscriptions. To encourage aggressive sales and market penetration, distributors receive a significantly higher revenue share during the first year of each school's subscription. This higher commission compensates distributors for their investment in sales, implementation, training, and customer relationship development. As schools continue renewing their subscriptions and customer acquisition costs decrease, the company's recurring revenue gradually increases while distributors continue benefiting from a growing customer portfolio.

Revenue sharing follows a configurable commission model managed by the centralized Commission Engine. During the first subscription year, distributors may receive approximately **70% of the recurring subscription revenue**, while the company retains the remaining **30%** to cover infrastructure, software maintenance, platform enhancement, payment processing, cloud hosting, customer support, and operational expenses. Upon the first renewal, the distributor's recurring revenue share automatically decreases to approximately **50%**, allowing the company to increase its recurring income while continuing to reward distributors for customer retention. From the third subscription year onward, the distributor's recurring share may further reduce to approximately **30%**, with the remaining revenue retained by the company. These percentages should never be hardcoded and must always be configurable through administration settings to support future commercial strategies, promotional campaigns, or market-specific pricing.

The platform distinguishes between subscription revenue and implementation revenue. Implementation, installation, data migration, onboarding, training, and deployment are operational services performed primarily by distributors and their agents. Schools may pay a one-time implementation fee at the beginning of the engagement, and this revenue is shared separately according to configurable business rules. The company may initially share a significant portion of implementation income with distributors to encourage customer acquisition while maintaining enough revenue to support technical assistance and platform onboarding. Since implementation requires considerable field effort, commission percentages for implementation services may differ from recurring subscription commissions.

The licensing model is equally configurable. Every distributor receives an annual business license authorizing them to market and support the platform within assigned territories. The first year may include significant discounts or promotional incentives to encourage partner onboarding and rapid network expansion. Agent registration also follows a licensing model where agents operate under distributors after obtaining authorization from the platform. Initial agent licensing may be offered at heavily discounted rates to encourage rapid field expansion, while renewal fees gradually normalize as the partner network matures. Every licensing policy, renewal rule, discount percentage, and validity period should remain configurable within the administration panel.

Distributor incentives are intentionally designed to reward customer acquisition and long-term retention rather than one-time sales. As distributors onboard additional schools, their recurring income grows through subscription commissions, implementation services, SMS package sales, premium feature activations, and future marketplace offerings. This recurring income transforms distributors from traditional software resellers into long-term business partners whose success is directly tied to the platform's continued growth. Since distributors continue earning from active subscriptions, they are naturally motivated to provide better customer service, improve retention, and maintain strong relationships with schools.

The company also supports configurable promotional pricing and discount management. Schools may receive promotional discounts during onboarding, seasonal campaigns, referral programs, educational initiatives, or strategic market expansion. During the first year, promotional discounts may be shared proportionally between the company and distributors to encourage competitive pricing without placing the entire financial burden on one party. As the partnership matures and renewal cycles begin, the company may gradually reduce its contribution toward promotional discounts, requiring distributors to absorb a greater portion of future discount costs if they choose to offer additional incentives. This gradual transition ensures that promotional activities remain sustainable while encouraging distributors to focus on value-based selling rather than continuous discounting.

Beyond subscriptions, the revenue model supports multiple complementary revenue streams that strengthen long-term profitability. Schools may purchase SMS packages, premium modules, additional user licenses, advanced analytics, AI-powered services, mobile applications, parent communication tools, custom reports, API integrations, and future marketplace extensions. Every commercial transaction should be processed through the centralized Financial Engine and should support independent pricing, commission rules, taxation, invoicing, discounts, promotional campaigns, and settlement calculations. This diversified revenue strategy reduces dependence on subscription income while creating additional earning opportunities for both the company and its partners.

The platform should support multiple subscription plans designed for institutions of varying sizes and operational requirements. Different plans may include Basic, Standard, Professional, Enterprise, or custom educational packages, each offering different combinations of modules, user limits, storage capacity, SMS quotas, support levels, implementation services, and premium features. Schools should also be able to purchase additional modules independently, enabling a modular pricing strategy where organizations pay only for the functionality they require. Module availability must be managed through the centralized Feature Engine, allowing administrators to activate or deactivate functionality dynamically based on subscription status.

Every financial transaction generated through subscriptions should pass through the centralized Financial Engine. Subscription creation, invoice generation, payment verification, commission calculation, distributor settlement, taxation, refunds, renewals, discounts, wallet adjustments, and financial reporting should all follow standardized accounting workflows. Individual modules must never maintain independent financial logic or calculate commissions directly. Instead, every commercial event should generate immutable ledger entries to ensure financial transparency, reconciliation, auditability, and regulatory compliance.

Payment processing should support multiple payment methods, including bank transfers, mobile financial services (such as bKash, Nagad, Rocket), online payment gateways, cards, and future digital payment channels. After successful payment verification, subscriptions should activate automatically according to configurable business rules. Failed payments, overdue invoices, expired subscriptions, and grace periods should also be configurable, allowing the company to define suspension policies, reminder schedules, and automatic renewal behavior without software changes.

Revenue distribution should occur through an automated Settlement Engine that calculates distributor earnings based on completed financial transactions. Settlements may be processed weekly, monthly, or according to configurable business schedules. Every settlement should include detailed invoice references, commission calculations, adjustment records, deductions, promotional offsets, tax calculations, SMS commissions, implementation earnings, and historical payment records. Distributors should have complete visibility into how their earnings are calculated through dedicated financial dashboards, while the Super Admin retains complete authority over approval, reconciliation, and payment processing.

As the platform expands, the Subscription and Revenue Model should remain adaptable to future business opportunities. The architecture should support franchise licensing, white-label partnerships, educational consortium pricing, government projects, reseller networks, educational NGOs, international pricing models, institution-specific contracts, AI-powered premium services, marketplace integrations, and regional pricing strategies without requiring major architectural changes. All pricing logic, commission structures, incentives, discounts, taxation rules, and commercial policies should be managed through configuration rather than application code.

Ultimately, the Subscription and Revenue Model serves as the financial backbone of the Amar School ERP ecosystem. It is designed not merely to collect subscription fees but to establish a sustainable recurring revenue platform that aligns the interests of the company, distributors, agents, and schools. By combining centralized financial governance with flexible commercial configuration, the platform creates a scalable business model capable of supporting thousands of institutions, hundreds of business partners, and millions of students while ensuring long-term profitability, operational efficiency, and continuous product innovation.
